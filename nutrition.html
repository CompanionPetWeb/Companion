The Full Reference

Everything your dog needs, in one place

If a food isn't listed here, play it safe and treat it as a "check with your vet first" — this covers the essentials, not every food out there.

Can my dog eat:
Protein Vitamin Mineral Carbohydrate Fiber Healthy Fat

Protein

Builds and repairs muscle, skin, and coat

Muscle meats
ChickenSkinless, cooked plain — no seasoning or bones
TurkeyLean and easy to digest, cooked plain
BeefLean cuts, cooked, fat trimmed off
DuckCooked, skin removed — richer, good for variety
RabbitLean and novel — good for sensitive stomachs
VenisonLean, cooked — common in limited-ingredient diets
Organ meats (moderation)
LiverRich in vitamin A — ~5–10% of diet max
KidneyNutrient-dense, cooked, small portions
HeartTechnically a muscle — lean, great occasional protein
Tripe (green)Naturally balanced calcium/phosphorus
Bones & chews (raw only)
Raw chicken feetNatural glucosamine/collagen for joints
Raw chicken necks/wingsSoft, edible raw bone — supervise always
Raw beef marrow bonesSize-appropriate, supervised
Raw turkey necksLarger alternative for bigger dogs
Seafood
SalmonCooked only, deboned — never raw or smoked
WhitefishCooked, deboned, plain
SardinesSoft bones are safe, packed in water not oil
ShrimpCooked, shell and tail removed
Eggs
Whole eggsFully cooked — scrambled or boiled, no salt
Avoid
Processed deli meatHigh salt and preservatives
Raw muscle meat/fishBacteria/parasite risk — raw bones are the exception
Cooked bonesSplinter easily — choking/gut injury risk
Fatty trims / skinCan trigger pancreatitis
Excess liver/organToo much vitamin A over time causes toxicity
General guide only — portion size depends on your dog's weight, age, and activity level. Always supervise bone-chewing. Check with a vet for exact amounts.

Vitamin

Supports immune health, vision, skin, and coat

Vegetables
CarrotsRaw or cooked — vitamin A (beta-carotene)
BroccoliSmall amounts — vitamin C and K
Sweet potatoCooked, no skin — vitamin A and fiber
SpinachSmall amounts, cooked — oxalates in excess
Bell peppersSeeds removed — high in vitamin C
PumpkinCooked, plain puree — vitamin A
CucumberRaw, sliced — low-calorie, hydrating snack
CelerySmall pieces — low-calorie, helps with fresh breath
ZucchiniRaw or cooked, plain — low-calorie
Green peasFresh or frozen, plain — not canned with added salt
Green beansPlain, cooked or raw — low-calorie fiber
CauliflowerSmall amounts, plain — can cause gas in excess
AsparagusCooked, plain — tough raw, easy to choke on
Brussels sproutsSmall amounts, cooked — can cause gas
CabbageSmall amounts, cooked — gas and thyroid effects in excess
Fruits
BlueberriesFresh or frozen — antioxidants
ApplesSeeds and core removed
WatermelonSeedless, rind removed
OrangesSmall amounts, peeled, seedless
StrawberriesFresh, sliced
BananaSmall amounts — potassium, high in natural sugar
PineappleFresh, core and rind removed, small amounts
MangoRipe, peeled and pitted — moderation, high in sugar
PearSeeds and core removed
CantaloupeSeedless, small amounts — high in sugar
PeachesFresh only, pitted — never canned/syrup
RaspberriesSmall amounts, occasional — contain trace natural xylitol
CranberriesPlain, small amounts — unsweetened only
Other sources
EggsCooked — vitamin A, D, B12, riboflavin
FishCooked — vitamin D and E
Plain yogurtNo xylitol — B vitamins and probiotics
Avoid
Grapes & raisinsToxic — acute kidney failure risk
Onions, garlic, chivesDamage red blood cells, even cooked
AvocadoPersin — skin and pit are the biggest risk
Unripe tomatoes / plantSolanine is toxic
Citrus peel & seedsCan upset the stomach
Raw / green potatoSolanine in raw or green-skinned potato is toxic
Wild mushroomsMany varieties are toxic — stick to plain cooked store-bought only
Whole foods are the best source — most dogs on a complete diet don't need separate vitamin supplements unless a vet recommends one.
💠

Mineral

Strengthens bones and teeth, supports nerve and muscle function

Great sources
Sardines (with bone)Calcium, phosphorus, omega-3
Yogurt / cottage cheeseSmall amounts — calcium, probiotics
Raw meaty bonesNecks/feet — raw only
Pumpkin seedsGround, small amount — zinc, magnesium
Eggshell (ground)A pinch — concentrated calcium
Seaweed / kelpTiny amount — iodine
Avoid
Calcium supplementsWithout guidance — risk for large-breed puppies
Too much organ meatCan throw off copper/iron balance
Human mineral supplementsNot dosed for a dog's size
Minerals work in ratios — too much of one can block absorption of another. Best from whole foods; supplement only under a vet's guidance.

Carbohydrate

Provides efficient, lasting energy

Great sources
White riceCooked plain — gentle on sensitive stomachs
Brown riceCooked — more fiber than white rice
OatsCooked plain — slow-release energy
Sweet potatoCooked, no skin
QuinoaCooked, rinsed well
BarleyCooked plain — fiber and B vitamins
Avoid
Bread & pastaRefined — some recipes hide garlic/onion
Corn on the cobChoking/blockage hazard
Sugary cerealsAdded sugar dogs don't need
Anything with xylitolToxic — check ingredient labels
Carbs aren't strictly essential the way protein and fat are, but they're a useful, efficient energy source in a balanced diet.
🌿

Fiber

Promotes healthy digestion and stool quality

Great sources
PumpkinPlain puree — helps firm up loose stool
Green beansLow-calorie, cooked or raw, plain
ApplesSeeds and core removed
OatsCooked plain — soluble fiber
Sweet potatoCooked, no skin
Leafy greensSpinach or kale, small amounts
Avoid
Too much, too fastCan cause gas or loose stool
Corn cobsNot digestible — real blockage risk
Human fiber cerealsOften contain raisins, chocolate, or xylitol
Introduce new fiber sources gradually — a sudden increase can upset a dog's stomach, even from a healthy food.

Healthy Fat

Supports skin, coat, brain, and joint health

Great sources
Salmon / fish oilCooked or vet-dosed — omega-3 EPA/DHA
Sardines & mackerelCooked or canned in water
Ground flaxseedSmall amount, fresh-ground
Avocado flesh (ripe)No skin/pit — many owners skip it entirely; skip if unsure
Chicken fat (rendered)Small amount — energy-dense
Avoid
Fried/greasy foodHigh pancreatitis risk
Bacon grease / trimmingsVery high fat load at once
Butter & excess oilCalorie-dense, little benefit
Macadamia nutsToxic — weakness, tremors, vomiting
Fat is calorie-dense — great in small amounts, but easy to overfeed. Introduce new fats gradually.
General guidance only — always confirm portions, routines, and health concerns with a veterinarian who knows your dog.
